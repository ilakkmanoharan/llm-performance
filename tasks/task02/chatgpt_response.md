# Tweet Thread

**Tweet 1:**  
Quantum mechanics can matter for perception — but that doesn’t mean consciousness is a quantum particle. Perception starts with quantum-scale events that classical brain processes later amplify.

**Tweet 2:**  
Vision begins when a single photon flips a retinal molecule. That first step is fully quantum, yet the resulting perception is classical neural computation.

**Tweet 3:**  
Some senses push the limits of quantum physics: shot noise in vision, molecular vibrations in smell, and even possible quantum effects in magnetoreception.

**Tweet 4:**  
These quantum events shape what we can detect — the thresholds, noise limits, and sensitivity of our senses — without implying the mind itself runs on quantum computing.

**Tweet 5:**  
In other words: quantum mechanics sets the inputs and physical limits of perception, while consciousness emerges from large-scale neural dynamics. No “consciousness particle” required.
